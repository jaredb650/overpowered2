# Torso gore proposal

Research only, 2026-11-16. Inspected the working `dev` checkout starting at HEAD `4dcde45c4dd8fa2b9c5c8f0bf855fc70b9564d18`, including fixes `2730b62` and `9008ff9`. File:line references below are relative to `overpowered-2/` and pinned to that source revision. There were unrelated working edits, including weapon files; concurrent FX work also appeared during research. This proposal changes no game code. Costs below are engineering estimates, not fresh frame-rate measurements.

## What happens today

### What makes a torso give

There are several different effects behind that description. A living humanoid does **not** have a chest or abdomen pool that bursts at a fixed damage total. Chest and abdomen normally take 1x health damage. Ordinary limb pools are `maxHealth * limbFrac * LIMB_SIZE`; that table contains arms and legs, not a human trunk. The exception is the drone's `abdomen` pool, at 30% of its health: that region describes a machine part, not a human waist. See `src/enemies/data.ts:385-392` and `src/enemies/EnemyManager.ts:274-279`.

| Trigger | Actual rule | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-body gib on death | Frozen bodies shatter. Otherwise a blown tank, a blast outcome of `gib`, or `bursts(d)` selects a gib when gibbing and explosive deaths are enabled. Boss death and drone crash have earlier branches; Lance vaporization also precedes an ordinary gib. | `src/enemies/damage.ts:785-808` |
| Direct explosive hit | `bursts(d)` means `d.gib === true`, or damage typed `explosive` with a struck `bone`. It still needs to kill; there is no additional universal torso damage threshold. | `src/enemies/wounds/rules.ts:7-13` |
| Explosion splash | Inside the fireball, damage at least equal to remaining health gives `gib`. The fireball ends at 25% of rocket radius, 22% of grenade radius, 30% of shell radius; a nuke uses the whole radius. Outside it, weighted maim/throw outcomes apply. | `src/enemies/blast.ts:67-88`, `src/enemies/blast.ts:137-162` |
| Blast waist tear | The maim ring can roll `waist`. Its full weight is reached at damage/maxHealth 0.9; below that weight falls quadratically, and below 0.45 it cannot be selected. | Weapon requests a gib | A shot can carry `gib`. For example, the Piledriver has a 0.3 shot probability, rolled into the shot specification and forwarded to damage. For ordinary limbs, living wear is `d.amount * severMult * SEVER_FACTOR[type] * faction.limbWear`: bullet 1, pellet 1.2, explosive 0.7, slash 2.4. That mechanism does not secretly supply a human torso pool (`src/enemies/damage.ts:140-143`, `src/enemies/damage.ts:541-555`). Nor is the wound fracture threshold a torso threshold: `WoundPlanner.strain` only accepts arm/leg bones; its base limit is 60 times size squared (`src/gore/wounds.ts:15-21`, `src/gore/wounds.ts:240-253`).

Wounds are another effect altogether. Bullets and pellets open shader holes and craters; a strong blast can make a crater up to 0.185 m radius at normal human size, plus burn and shrapnel marks. Wounds merge and deepen. They expose an interior, but do not release corresponding pieces of torso. See `src/gore/wounds.ts:162-179`, `src/gore/wounds.ts:196-220`, and `docs/notes/characters.md:192-224`.

### What happens to the mesh

**Full gib:** `Gore.gib` samples the body's pose, takes available head/arm/leg chains, creates their loose ragdolls, and hides those chains. It spawns the flesh burst, destroys any owned corpse ragdoll, and calls `char.release()` (`src/gore/index.ts:1364-1429`). The character library then removes the original root from the scene, resets it and returns it to the pool (`src/characters/CharacterLibrary.ts:228-235`). The torso is neither cut into matching pieces nor swapped for a broken torso. It is removed with the source character.

**Waist sever:** `abdomen` maps to the severable spine bone. Its subtree includes chest, head and both arms; the legs are children of hips and stay on the original instance (`src/characters/rig.ts:94-115`, `src/characters/rig.ts:136-158`, `src/gore/rigInfo.ts:60-65`). `Gore.sever` copies that subtree into a pooled `SeveredPart`, cuts its parts out of any existing ragdoll, then hides the source subtree (`src/gore/index.ts:1021-1079`). `Character.hideLimb` scales the source joint to `COLLAPSED = 1e-5`, marks descendants hidden, and enables the baked parent cap. The copied chain uses its baked chain indices and child cap. This is a real two-piece separation, achieved with prebuilt geometry and bones, not runtime torso fracture (`src/characters/Character.ts:13`, `src/characters/Character.ts:274-286`, `src/characters/Character.ts:611-668`).

The remaining original hips/legs can become a ragdoll, while the upper half is a separate `Rag`. `Rag` creates bodies for bones marked `body`, merges other bone colliders onto them, and joins adjacent bodies (`src/gore/rag.ts:373-415`, `src/gore/rag.ts:449-505`). An intact humanoid is 11 rigid bodies: hips, chest, head, two upper arms, two forearms, two thighs and two shins. A waist separation divides those into five below and six above; it does not require two complete 11-body skeletons.

**Sword slice:** there is also a genuine posed-mesh clipping path. `SliceField.cut` calls `bakePose`, cuts against a plane, caps the results, and gives each disconnected piece one convex-hull body with a box fallback. Those pieces are rigid snapshots, not articulated halves that can crawl (`src/gore/sliced.ts:132-140`, `src/gore/sliced.ts:171-204`). It is useful reference code for caps, not a reason to perform three to six expensive runtime cuts on every gib. The notes also record that slices lose held props and loose limbs cannot be severed further (`docs/notes/gore.md:606-610`).

### What flies out

For a flesh full gib, `fleshBurst` creates **13 meat, five viscera, five bone** pieces. Meat starts around the chest, viscera around the hips, and bone around the chest. Their size parameters are 0.05–0.13 m, 0.06–0.10 m and 0.03–0.055 m respectively; these scale unit geometries and are not full widths. Launch speed is force/30 clamped to 6–15 m/s before the force dial, with random directional/upward variation (`src/gore/index.ts:1386-1388`, `src/gore/index.ts:1432-1466`).

Those three shapes are displaced icosahedra, stretched for viscera and bone. Meat and viscera each have 80 triangles; bone has 20, giving 1,540 visible chunk triangles per full flesh burst. Meat is dark red, viscera pink-red, bone cream. They already use flat-shaded `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, vertex colours, low roughness and clearcoat; meat is roughness 0.3/clearcoat 0.7, viscera 0.2/1, bone 0.55/0.15. These are wet-looking procedural lumps, not grey placeholder cubes, but none has a recognizable rib cage, vertebra or retained chest shell (`src/gore/chunks.ts:56-80`, `src/gore/chunks.ts:231-235`, `src/gore/chunks.ts:255-266`).

They are **real Rapier debris**, each with one dynamic body and cuboid collider, colliding only with the world. Restitution is 0.32 and friction 0.75. Once still for 0.35 seconds, or after seven seconds in flight, the body is removed and the instance stays. After 14 more seconds it shrinks over 0.8 seconds. Slots and rendering are pooled; rigid bodies are currently created and removed, not retained as a reusable disabled-body pool (`src/gore/chunks.ts:17-31`, `src/gore/chunks.ts:290-355`, `src/gore/chunks.ts:374-408`).

The head and four limbs are separate surviving character pieces. Each arm/leg has a 35% chance to use its lower joint instead; already missing chains are skipped. Thus a whole head, two arms/forearms and two legs/shins can fly away, while the unselected proximal flesh disappears with the original trunk. Full limbs use two bodies each, lower limbs one, and the head one: **five chains with five to nine bodies**, on top of the 23 small chunks, for **28–32 active bodies from one intact flesh gib** before caps retire anything. The head is thrown whole here; the nine-piece skull/jaw/brain/eye kit belongs to the separate head-burst path (`src/gore/index.ts:201-205`, `src/gore/index.ts:1397-1422`, `src/gore/chunks.ts:176-191`).

Loose limbs keep skin, wounds, caps and attached props. They have a three-second `bloodTrail`, a short cut-end `stumpSpray`, droplets along flight, landing stains and a pool on freezing (`src/gore/index.ts:982-985`, `src/gore/index.ts:1105-1128`, `src/gore/index.ts:1950-1993`). Small chunks get up to three floor splats when they lose speed sharply and organ landing sounds, but no continuous blood trail and no sticking rule (`src/gore/chunks.ts:385-399`). Machine gibs use 12 scrap pieces and sparks instead of flesh (`src/gore/index.ts:1469-1479`).

### Blood, decals, and the disappearing read

A flesh gib already has a substantial blood treatment: `fx.gibBurst`, three `bloodJet(..., 2)` calls, 18 queued splatter rays reaching seven metres, 14 invisible ballistic decal droplets, and a 1.4–2.1 m pool growing over 1.6 seconds (`src/gore/index.ts:1457-1466`). `fx.gibBurst` supplies 21 strands/drops and two mist elements; each intensity-2 jet adds ten strands, two late drops and a mist element. The combined recipe is 62 visible blood elements, not additional solid gibs, plus near-camera lens spatter (`src/fx/recipes/blood.ts:55-67`, `src/fx/recipes/blood.ts:93-113`).

FX predicts floor/wall landings and books directional splats. Gore separately tracks invisible droplets against actual world rays, queues surface splats, and grows pools in five decal stages. Its update budget is 24 rays, 160 droplets, 96 queued splats and 24 pool records; pool setup can also cast synchronously, up to the separate twice-budget guard. Droplet impacts now use directional `decals.splat`, while queued splats still use randomly rotated `decals.add` (`src/gore/blood.ts:13-26`, `src/gore/blood.ts:159-179`, `src/gore/blood.ts:189-215`, `src/gore/blood.ts:219-244`). FX has a separate 192-entry stain queue, and world decals use a 2,048-instance ring (`src/fx/stains.ts:5`, `src/fx/stains.ts:49-56`, `src/render/Decals.ts:6`). The gore ray budget is not a global cap on every FX ray.

The disappearing impression follows directly from that sequence:

1. The largest continuous shape, the clothed torso, leaves the scene in the gib call. Nothing with its outline replaces it.
2. Twenty-three small, similar lumps cannot communicate where the chest, back and pelvis went. They spawn near two points rather than opening out from matching surfaces.
3. Head and limbs retain recognizable silhouettes, which makes the missing middle more noticeable. A detached upper half later bursting into six generic pieces repeats the same problem.
4. The blood sells impact, then clears to show scattered small pieces. More spray would conceal the transition briefly; it would not provide the missing structure.

This is a code-based diagnosis. No new motion capture or owner-screen performance test was run for this proposal. Some FX notes are historical: for example, the recommendation to call `ChunkField.ensure()` at startup is already implemented in the constructor (`docs/notes/fx.md:176-194`, `src/gore/index.ts:371-377`).

### 1. Crack into chunks: the main full-gib treatment

Start with **four large pieces**: left chest/ribs, right chest/ribs, rear chest/spine, and abdomen/pelvis. A six-piece recipe can separate upper chest and lower abdomen later. The pieces should occupy the original torso before they move, leaving the viewer able to track the mass. Bias the broken faces toward the camera through their initial rotation, without making every event identical. Give a bullet-driven burst a directional opening; let a blast spread them away from its centre.

Precompute seams and closed caps in the bake pipeline. A fracture asset needs outer skin/cloth, a thick flesh cross-section, exposed bone and a cheap collider for each piece. Reuse bone/organ construction and flesh shading as source material, but export closed geometry: the existing interior is only intended to show under wound masks and is not a watertight detachable chunk (`src/characters/anatomy/build.ts:1-19`). Give each seam a small jagged overlap to prevent a visible daylight crack before separation.

`SectionName` currently names binary **attribute/index sections**, not anatomical pieces: `position`, `lod0`, `caps`, `chains`, and so on. `GoreRegion` names damage regions, not six fracture chunks. The `PartId` in `src/characters/implants.ts:26` names implant dressings (`ImplantId | 'core'`), and the other `PartId` is for vehicles. None is an existing torso-fracture registry (`src/characters/format.ts:12-40`, `src/characters/rig.ts:12-15`, `src/enemies/vehicles/data.ts:11`). Add explicit torso recipe metadata, initially as separate baked props/manifest entries, rather than forcing ribs and pelvis into those unrelated types. Version the binary format only if chunks are later embedded into it.

At the event, obtain all replacement slots first, seed their world transforms from hips/chest and the current pose, then release the original. Precomputed bone-local pieces need a defined seam frame and pose handling: sample the skinned boundary into preallocated buffers or blend a narrow seam ring from its contributing bones. A bind-pose assembly dropped onto a bent ragdoll would pop visibly. Limit the first prototype to four pieces aligned to hips/chest and test crouched, prone and twisted bodies before expanding it.

Use one body per large piece, one box or precomputed convex collider, world-only collisions, no chunk-to-chunk contacts, and no fracture solver. Skin and wet cap may need two draws unless a shared material handles their different surface response. Keep that cost explicit. The current 23 small physical chunks should be **replaced**, not retained underneath the new pieces.

### 2. Tear in half: improve the path already present

Keep the existing articulated upper and lower halves, baked parent/child caps and inherited velocities. Add a short vertebra/spine end to each cap and two or three low-sided strands that sag, stretch and detach after roughly 0.15–0.35 seconds. These should be skinned ribbons or small tubes in one batch, updated from endpoint transforms. They should not be a dozen Rapier bodies connecting two heavy ragdolls. Fade/snap before the gap becomes implausibly long; after that one end can trail briefly from a half.

The upper half already crawls for about 4.9–8.4 seconds when severed before gore owns the body. It pulls with its arms through `Crawler`; a waist cut on an already owned body is inert (`src/gore/index.ts:1051-1053`, `src/gore/index.ts:1097-1102`, `src/gore/crawl.ts:39-63`). In a lethal waist path this is a gore after-death performance, not a living AI enemy that can still attack. Keep that distinction. A genuinely surviving upper-body enemy would require health, targeting, attack and network ownership work and is outside the recommended first build.

Preserve `2730b62`: `canCrawl` requires flesh and matching humanoid bone names; a drone's abdomen gun must never start a human crawler (`src/gore/index.ts:193-199`). Here “humans only” means flesh humanoid anatomy, which includes humanoid swarm characters; it does not mean just Outpost soldiers. Keep the missing-bone fallback in `Rag.partFor` as well (`src/gore/rag.ts:800-808`). Repeated damage to an upper half should shed a recognizable chest/back chunk before its final disappearance, replacing the generic six-piece burst.

### 3. Blow-out: use the existing wound geometry first

There is already a skin-discard shader, backing and a fitted rib cage, spine, pelvis and organs. At LOD 0 an opened body adds interior and backing draws; LOD 1 uses backing only and LOD 2 omits wounds (`src/characters/Character.ts:590-608`, `docs/notes/characters.md:194-206`). Start by tuning one large crater/exit with a broken rib edge, a dark thick rim and two recognizable expelled kit pieces. The existing shader marks can make a hole through a surface; a flat blood decal alone cannot create a cavity or change a silhouette.

Use baked partial-torso stand-ins for a missing side of a rib cage later. The partial-head pipeline is a useful asset/loading precedent, but `Character.standIn` collapses one bone's skin contribution. A torso spans several bones with child limbs, so blindly applying the head substitution to chest/spine would produce stretched residual skin or missing attachments. It needs an explicit torso skin mask and preserved child transforms (`src/characters/Character.ts:384-394`, `docs/notes/gore.md:89-100`).

This is best for a strong hit that leaves a torso behind. It should not replace every full gib: the owner's request also needs bodies that visibly break into large pieces.

### 4. Better kit: the fastest useful improvement

Author a curved rib section with red tissue on its inner edge, a short vertebra stack with a marrow end, a dark liver-like mass, a pale folded bowel shape, and a torn cloth strip. Build deterministic seeded variants at load or bake time. The existing head kit already starts recognizable pieces where they belonged inside a head; repeat that idea inside a chest (`src/gore/chunks.ts:83-191`, `src/gore/chunks.ts:269-287`). Reuse the current material patcher so pieces take the same light and fog as the world.

Reuse chunk landing sounds/splats and selected `bloodTrail` emission. Instanced chunks have no individual scene object to attach an emitter to; either provide a small reusable proxy for the selected hero pieces or feed sampled transforms directly into blood emission. Do not attach emitters to every fragment. Keep existing speed-loss splats for floor impacts; for a convincing wall stick, use a bounded contact/sweep query that supplies the actual wall point and normal, stop the body, and retain a static instance there. Sticking is a new feature, not something `stainBelow` currently does.

Soft organs should bounce little and settle, bone should tumble, cloth should flutter without a collider. Spend specular highlights on flesh, keep bone less glossy, and use strong shape differences rather than a brighter red on every piece. Support the large torso chunks with two small physical pieces and several cheap visual fragments; this kit on its own improves gibs but does not conserve the torso silhouette.

### 5. Armour: make the existing damage order visible

Plates already have HP, geometry and separate meshes. Damage can stop on a plate, break it, or penetrate it before health/wounds are resolved. `shootOff` launches the plate; `tearOff` handles penetrating rounds/charges and preserves boss-specific restrictions (`src/enemies/damage.ts:399-435`, `src/enemies/damage.ts:629-645`, `src/enemies/damage.ts:690-698`). `PlateDebris` is currently a fake ballistic arc with zero rigid bodies, capped at 24 flying meshes and lasting 1.1–1.7 seconds (`src/enemies/armor.ts:177-205`).

Use that event to swap to two or three pre-cut plate fragments, with a brief crack/rotation cue, before or in the same frame as the underlying flesh breaks. Do not delay authoritative damage for a staged animation. Current plates sit over baked armour, so dropping the outer plate can leave another armoured surface behind. A matching exposed-cloth/flesh wound or baked variant is needed if the effect is supposed to reveal bare anatomy (`src/enemies/armor.ts:1-7`). Keep existing plate colours and low-cost flight as the default; a new physics body for every shard would add little.

## Weapon rules and co-op

**AP/HP:** preserve the current damage model. AP uses 0.8x flesh damage, passes 75% of the resulting amount through ordinary plates, and wears them by the round; HP uses 1.5x flesh damage but a plate stops it and only takes 15% round wear (`src/weapons/data.ts:17-25`, `src/weapons/mods.ts:34-43`, `src/enemies/damage.ts:204-209`). Ammo class should choose wound character after armour has answered: narrow AP channel/larger far-side exit, wider shallow HP damage against exposed flesh. Those distinct shapes are proposed work: `WoundPlanner` currently chooses its bullet bite by weapon source, not `load` (`src/gore/wounds.ts:314-316`). HP should not crack a protected chest merely because its unarmoured multiplier is large, and AP is not the same flag as `DamageInfo.pierces`.

**Raufoss:** preserve its direct hit, nearby burst and exit cone as one compound shot. The direct round is 1.25x damage; the standard small burst is radius 1.3 m/damage 22, followed by four fragments each doing 0.4x base damage, cone 0.09 rad, force 14. If armour took the round and the target lives, its fragments are applied inside with `pierces`, rather than fired behind him (`src/weapons/raufoss.ts:48-58`, `src/weapons/raufoss.ts:78-121`, `src/weapons/data.ts:859-867`). It does not universally turn the direct hit into `explosive`, and its 14-force fragments do not meet the 100-force waist rule. A source shot can still carry its existing gib request. Aggregate cosmetic injury by shot/target so direct hit, burst and four fragments produce one torso transition, not six conflicting spawns. Use the shot direction for the exit-side cavity and ejected kit.

**Bullets versus blasts:** leave ordinary rounds making localized channels/craters and accumulating the existing health/limb/corpse damage. Use whole torso chunks when the damage model has selected a full gib; retain the waist outcome for suitable pellets and blast-ring rolls. A blast should produce several different outcomes across a crowd, as its existing table intends. A special torso fracture damage pool would be a separate balance change, not required to fix presentation.

**Lance:** keep sag, rise, glow and ash as its distinct death. Its vaporize branch precedes ordinary full-gib selection unless frozen; the current concurrent limit is ten and duration is 1.6–2.3 seconds, with immediate ash when full (`src/enemies/damage.ts:790-807`, `src/gore/index.ts:50-63`, `src/gore/index.ts:1499-1535`). Do not layer flesh chunks onto vaporized kills. Small non-vaporizing plasma hits can retain their crater/scorch. Boss/drone special deaths retain their existing precedence.

**Co-op:** `9008ff9` already gives the host authoritative damage and broadcasts wound presentation (`hitfx`), sever and kill-with-gib events. Wounds are replayed at a fixed visual severity of 0.6, not as an exact serialized wound book; snapshots also repair severed regions. The protocol does not send torso recipe IDs, random seeds or debris transforms (`src/net/world.ts:52-57`, `src/net/world.ts:157-180`, `src/net/world.ts:250-252`, `src/net/worldProtocol.ts:17-25`). Host damage admission still runs through `applyDamage` (`src/net/world.ts:122-131`).

Extend that event lane with an authoritative torso outcome, recipe/version, seed, shot/event ID, original pose frame and impulse direction. Send the chosen break and missing-part mask once; guests must not reroll the death outcome. Deduplicate hit/sever/kill events for a single transition, including the existing per-descendant sever notifications. A client with a lower visual budget may omit small pieces while preserving the same large missing body regions. Debris trajectories can remain client-local Rapier/ballistic effects; deterministic seeds choose shapes but do not guarantee identical physics. Do not stream dozens of chunk transforms at 10 Hz. If torso halves become gameplay actors later, their positions and targets need authoritative replication; today's visual crawler is not that system. Add a corpse visual ID/lifetime if post-death torso breaks are to replicate, since current event lookup drops a replica on kill.

## Pooling and an explosion budget

Current limits are useful but do not bound the combined cost. `ChunkField` has 304 visual slots over eight kinds, 72 active small-chunk bodies and eight instanced meshes. Gore separately permits 22 live loose-limb **chains**, 48 retained limbs, 40 corpses and a target of 14 active corpse ragdolls (`src/gore/chunks.ts:17-21`, `src/gore/index.ts:65-119`). A chain is several bodies. The ragdoll target is intentionally soft while bodies are airborne/fresh, and full gibs create pieces before enforcing limb limits. The blast's 12-cut counter does not wrap the full-gib loop. Thus “72 chunks” or “12 cuts” does not mean one explosion cannot create hundreds of rigid bodies (`src/gore/index.ts:1397-1429`, `src/gore/index.ts:915-948`, `src/enemies/damage.ts:491-496`, `src/enemies/damage.ts:865-870`).

Use these **proposed starting caps**, then adjust from target-machine captures:

| Resource | Initial budget and fallback |
|---|---|
| Dead-body and cosmetic physics | Hard ceiling of 64 active rigid bodies across dead `Rag`s, loose limbs, torso chunks, small chunks, sliced pieces and vaporizing corpses. Count bodies, not chains. Reserve before constructing, waking or converting anything. Living knockdowns stay a separate gameplay reservation. |
| One explosion | At most 48 newly activated physics bodies, counting its admitted living knockdowns as well as deaths and debris. Repeated Raufoss/secondary events also share the global ceiling. A normal uncut humanoid ragdoll costs eleven credits. Prioritize living reactions before decorative debris. |
| Near full gib | Four torso bodies + two kit bodies + at most nine surviving head/limb bodies = fifteen. Three full nearby gibs cost at most 45 when no other blast reactions consume the allowance. Further victims use two to four torso/limb silhouette proxies with ballistic motion and zero new bodies. Blood and authoritative deaths still happen. |
| Torso piece slots | 32 visible large pieces, shared by all recipes; 96 small kit slots; 24 plate-fragment slots. Reclaim old settled/out-of-view pieces first. Keep an emergency coarse torso substitute so a pool miss never leaves an empty centre. |
| Activation work | Queue and activate at most eight new decorative bodies per frame. While waiting, already-visible pieces move ballistically from their seeded pose. Never hide the source until its visual substitutes exist. Precompute geometry/colliders, warm shaders at load, and preallocate chunk records, transforms and proxy meshes. Pool disabled simple debris bodies where Rapier reset semantics allow it; articulated ragdoll pooling needs separate care. |
| Long-running effects | At most eight new torso/kit trail emitters globally, chosen by size and proximity, inside the existing 64-emitter pool. One or two trail sources per hero event is enough. Strands have zero bodies, short lifetimes and one shared batch. |
| Stains and queries | Keep the 160-droplet/96-queued/24-pool gore limits and 2,048 world decal ring. Allocate new contact/sweep and trail landing queries from a shared 24-query allowance; cache/coalesce FX floor/wall samples and move synchronous pool casts into admission or the queue. At most three impact splats per hero piece. The existing separate FX queries must be included before calling this a hard budget. |
| Rendering and memory | Aim for at most 16 additional visible batch draws and 30k active torso/kit/strand triangles across the scene, before existing body skins/backings. Reuse kits across archetypes; load torso variants only for the contract's roster. Budget up to 8 MB incremental geometry/instance buffers for the first roster, measured separately from any new texture atlases. |

These are shared allowances, not additive pools of 64 + 72 + 22 chains. Waking an old corpse or transferring an already active ragdoll must debit only the bodies actually added and release credits for destroyed bodies. Include sliced and vaporizing bodies in accounting; at a full allowance use the proposed coarse proxy or the existing vaporization-to-ash fallback rather than silently bypassing it. The 64 ceiling will reduce the effective number of fully articulated corpses below fourteen in a busy scene; that is a deliberate proposed performance tradeoff.

Do not achieve a cap by freezing a freshly thrown body in mid-air. Existing notes explain why that failed. Use a moving, nonphysical proxy for new lower-priority debris, or retire already settled pieces. A low-detail death proxy needs a cached fall/tumble pose and a floor/world sweep so it reaches the ground rather than holding its standing pose. Living knockdowns that cannot be admitted should use the existing stagger/animated fall fallback; a decorative budget must not freeze an active enemy. Returning proxies/bodies must clear emitters, wound state, scales, timers and collision masks so reused pieces cannot inherit someone else's blood stream or hidden pose.

## What we can reuse

- `Gore` ownership and single-death flow, animation velocity seeds, `Rag.cut`, limb pools, sever caps and `Crawler`: most of the waist split already exists.
- `ChunkField` instancing, wet material patching, world-only collision setup, settling, sounds, stains and head-kit layout: extend these for recognizable torso debris instead of starting another particle/debris engine.
- The fitted anatomy builder for proportions and bone/organ shapes; the slice cap's textured wet flesh for wound-face treatment. Detachable assets still need closed caps and appropriate materials.
- Bake pipeline and prop/manifest loading, plus partial heads as a stand-in asset precedent. Preserve different body proportions and contract-specific suit colours rather than attaching one generic naked torso to every enemy.
- Existing wound shader/backing/interior for localized blow-outs, and `PlateDebris` for cheap equipment breakage.
- Reconcile concurrent FX work before implementation: working additions seen during this research include `bloodSpray`, `bloodDrip` and wall-running decals. Reuse those if they land, rather than building a second spray/drip/run path. They are outside the pinned baseline above and were not validated as completed features here.
- Existing host damage/event lane and sandbox/test harnesses. Add torso recipe data and admission accounting there; keep authoritative combat separate from cosmetic trajectories.

## Recommendation

Build **1 + 4** as the full-gib result: four substantial, capped torso pieces and a small recognizable support kit. Start with **4** to improve both full gibs and the particularly weak six-piece upper-half burst quickly, then prove **1** on one archetype before paying for the roster. Polish **2** in parallel with that rollout as a distinct waist-tear outcome, retaining its existing limited crawl. Use **5** wherever plates already exist. Keep **3** for localized damage that leaves a torso behind, starting with the wound/interior system already in the game.

The visual goal is straightforward: after the spray clears, the player can point to the chest, back and lower trunk that just broke apart. Budget for those large shapes first.
