# The campaign story: the gist

Status: the owner's direction, 2026-11-14, written down so every mission is built against one story. The gist, not a script.
Each mission's own plan (`MISSION.md` for the Outpost, the Foreclosure rewrite next) must fit inside this.

## The world

**The Bureau.** The Office of Societal Compliance: an intergalactic bureaucracy nobody has seen. It does not hate anyone and
does not want anything. A form is filed, the form is processed, and a people stop existing. Nobody at the Bureau knows why the
form was filed, and nobody asks. Kafka, *Brazil*: a man is executed because a fly fell into a typewriter, except here it is a
planet. Its voice is the Council: deadpan, on a recorded line, billing you for everything.

**You.** A contractor. Not one man: a product. You are cloned, and when you die the next one is issued and the fee deducted.
Mission 1 already hints at it: "You again. Or another one of you. Does it matter?", "Replacement contractor issued", and the
previous contractor's wreck whose launcher you take.

## What the player does not know

Each mission feels like a separate job somewhere in a big galaxy: kill this commander, put down this uprising, hit this bank.

The truth: **every mission is on the same planet, inside about 48 hours, and there are thousands of you working at once.**
The Bureau is not punishing anyone. It is deleting a whole people, one system at a time:

| Day | System | Mission |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The military | 1, The Outpost (built): kill the Commander and his garrison |
| 1 | Trade | 2, Foreclosure (being rewritten): the port of Holdfast rises up; the Bureau sends an army, and you |
| 1 to 2 | The dead | 3, The Sump (planned, the zombie mission). "Deceased personnel remain eligible to serve" |
| 2 | Money | 4, the central bank (idea: the bank robbery) |
| 2 | Power | 5, the grid: the news reveal plays here (below) |
| later | Everything else | to be decided |

## How it is revealed

Mostly through the world, a little more each mission:

- **News on screens and radios** in the levels, worse every time: "Military command unreachable." "Ports burning in eleven
  cities." "Day 2."
- **Wanted posters**: one suspect, glowing red goggles.
- **Enemies who have seen the news** react to you: "It's him. It's the goggles." The Harbourmaster in Foreclosure is the first
  to notice something is wrong: "You're the one from the news. You were also at Port Vell this morning. And Carrow. How are you
  in three places?"
- **Around mission 5**, just before you blow the grid, a broadcast lays it out: the same man sighted in 48 countries in two
  days, banks, ports, every military command, one suspect. A map fills with red dots. Every dot is you.
- **Late**, maybe the end: the reason, as a receipt. Possibly the notice was meant for a different planet. Nobody at the
  Bureau cares either way.

## Tone

Unserious on top, bleak underneath. Funny villains, dry Council, huge violence. Cyberpunk, giga-capitalist, giga-bureaucratic,
nihilist: everything atomised and disconnected, people trying to deal with something they cannot argue with, reason with or
even find.

## How a mission is built (learned from the Outpost and the first Foreclosure)

The Outpost worked; the first Foreclosure did not. The difference, in the owner's words, is what every mission keeps:

- **A villain with personality who controls the level** and reacts to you: the Commander shuts the gate, jams his own tank in
  it, kills the lights, never paid for the bridge. Every section opens with them doing something to you.
- **Set pieces that change how you play** (no damage on the tanks until the launcher, sonar in the dark, wall-running the
  missing walkway), not just more men to shoot.
- **Dialogue that says plainly what is happening and what to do.** World-building is flavour on top, never the only way to
  understand the job.
- **Real places on a clear route**: walls you cannot jump, sight lines that hide what comes next, landmarks you remember.
- **Big tools need big fights**: a gunship section has an army under it.
- **One story**: every mission plan is checked against this page before it is built.
